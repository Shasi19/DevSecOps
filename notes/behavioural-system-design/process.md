# Behavioural and system-design interview practice process

## 1. Build a truthful experience inventory

List projects/incidents across delivery, reliability, security, conflict, ambiguity, failure, prioritization, customer impact, mentorship, and learning. Record your role, constraints, actions, result, and what changed afterward. Do not invent ownership or metrics.

## 2. Prepare STAR stories

For each experience: Situation in two sentences; Task/your responsibility; Actions with decisions, collaboration, and trade-offs; Result with substantiated evidence; Reflection on learning. Practice adapting one story to different competencies without changing facts.

## 3. Practice system design

Use a 35–45 minute timer:
1. Clarify users, functional/nonfunctional requirements, scale, and constraints.
2. State assumptions and rough traffic/storage estimates.
3. Draw simplest architecture and data flow.
4. Define API/data model and consistency.
5. Explore bottlenecks, failure modes, security, privacy, observability, deployment, and recovery.
6. Compare options; summarize trade-offs and unresolved risks.

## 4. Review and improve

Record a practice session; assess structure, correctness, collaboration, listening, assumptions, and time. Ask a peer for specific feedback. Maintain a revision sheet; do not memorize scripts that prevent adapting to a prompt.

## 5. During the interview

Clarify ambiguous requirements, narrate reasoning, invite correction, acknowledge uncertainty, and use examples from actual experience. For system design, optimize only after explaining baseline and constraints. For behavioural prompts, take responsibility and avoid blaming colleagues.

## Afterward

Record questions and learning while fresh, but do not retain confidential interview content or proprietary assessment material beyond permitted policy.
